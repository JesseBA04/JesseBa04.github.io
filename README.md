## Digital Equity and Technology Access for Underserved Communities

The computer is one of the most important inventions of all time. And since its inception there have been upgrades and updates to every system possible, allowing nearly the entire globe to be connected to each other. Although everyone is connected, this doesn't mean that everything is equal. There is still widespread poverty, people living in the same city can have radically different lives simply because they were born into different families. The internet can allow anyone to find the help that they need in order to lift themselves up again. This is why I believe in digital equity and technology access for underserved communities.

Through my time here at the University of St. Thomas, I've been able to learn more about what it means to be all for the Common Good. Through classes such as my Philosophy of Social Justice, I've been able to learn about thinkers and people who lived their entire lives theorizing how do we make this world a better and more equitable place for everyone to live. I believe that my skills in Computer Science are an invaluable asset to this fight, as the world has become connected thanks to the internet, the things I create are able to impact people whom I will never meet.

Digital equity is important to the Common Good because access to technology today is no longer an option. The Common Good refers to:

> "...facilities – whether material, cultural, or institutional – that the members of a community provide to all members in order to fulfill a relational obligation they all have to care for certain interests they have in common."
> — *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*

By this definition, the Common Good can only be achieved by helping make sure that everyone in a community is given equitable access to things, and the best way to connect those who need the help is through the internet.

## Capstone Project — Minnesota Alliance on Crime

My Capstone project with the Minnesota Alliance on Crime is a great example of how I attempted to work to help create digital equity. The goal of this project was to help connect victims of crime to organizations that would be able to offer them the help they needed, such as shelter, legal resources, and mental health care to name a few. During this project I was able to grow as a leader. At first, we struggled deciding certain things as we had no prominent voice that was able to guide discussion. This led to me taking the role, which allowed me to gain a better understanding of what we needed to accomplish and who we needed to accomplish it for — victims of crime in the state of Minnesota. I started this project at the same time as my Philosophy of Social Justice course.

My Philosophy class allowed me to really understand many societal issues, and how best to address them. And in connection with my Capstone project, I was able to better understand why what we were doing was important not only for ourselves but for the community of Minnesota as a whole, as no resource had previously existed like this in the state. The lack of resources in Minnesota helped me better understand the lack of digital equity for victims of crime, as many states simply don't have very robust database systems to allow people to find the resources they need. This project helped open my eyes; there are many issues in society that are not due to an apparatus of dominance purposely putting others down, although there still are many of those, but there are plenty of issues that happen and aren't fixed because there is no one available, or that cares, to fix them. We can only gain this type of insight when we take the time to solve an issue and attempt to better understand why certain things happen.

During this project I was also able to interact with our community partner. This experience allowed me to learn many things, such as how to interpret difficult concepts into more concise language, and how to present a product to a client and attempt to gain input in areas where we're looking for some. My ability to interpret grew a lot from this experience, mostly from skills gained in other courses here at St. Thomas such as philosophy and museum studies, which forced me to think about difficult concepts and interpret them into ways that made sense to me. Additionally, when presenting, I was able to gain experience not only showing off the product but also asking questions in areas where we knew our knowledge lacked and wanted input for our next steps within specific areas.

## Database Design Project — Local Food Shelf

Another project I worked on was my Database design project, where I was a part of a team that created a database system for a local food shelf. During development we struggled to figure out how wide in scope we wanted to create this project, as we had been given a lot of freedom to figure out what we wanted to do. This forced us as a group to have a discussion to better understand what we wanted to accomplish and how we wanted each part to look within the schema. Through these discussions we were able to all gain a better understanding of what we wanted to accomplish and how we saw the project evolving in the future. This project would be able to help a large food bank in St. Paul be able to more efficiently serve their community and many local families.
